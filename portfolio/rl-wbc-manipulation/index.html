    Learning-Based Whole-Body Manipulation with a Quadruped Mobile Manipulator

    Graduate Researcher · Mar. 2023 – Feb. 2025
    Hanyang University · HRC Lab

    Overview

    This project explored a hybrid learning-and-control architecture for dynamic manipulation of a passively connected object with a quadruped mobile manipulator.

    Simulated full-body execution of the learned swing-up trajectory.

    Approach

    An RL agent generated swing-up trajectories in a reduced-order cart-pole model. The learned trajectory generator was then integrated with a whole-body controller that coordinated contact, body, center-of-mass, and end-effector objectives on the full robot.

    My work focused on integrating the learned trajectory generator with whole-body tracking and evaluating the resulting feasibility gap between the reduced-order plan and the full-body execution.

    Architecture

    The trajectory generator was trained with Soft Actor-Critic on a cart-pole abstraction using the cart and passive-joint states. Its output was converted into end-effector motion commands for an optimization-based whole-body controller that coordinated contact constraints, body orientation, center-of-mass motion, and end-effector tracking.

    Paper Poster Publication record

    Demo

    Joint project demonstration: an RL-generated end-effector swing-up trajectory tracked by the quadruped mobile manipulator.

    Research Insight

    Simulation verified swing-up of the passive joint and full-body trajectory tracking. The project also highlighted a practical limitation of modular learning-and-control pipelines: a trajectory can solve the abstract task while remaining difficult for a contact-constrained, high-dimensional robot to execute. This observation motivates tighter coupling between learned planning and whole-body feasibility.
